Cost Guide

What land clearing and forestry mulching cost in Alabama

We won't put a price list on a page and pretend it fits your property. What we will do is show you exactly what moves the number up and down, and how to read a quote without getting caught out.

Get a number for your property

First, the honest part

We are not going to put a price list on this page and pretend it applies to your property. It wouldn't. Two five-acre tracts a mile apart can be three times apart in cost, and the difference is almost never the acreage — it's what's standing on it, how steep it is, how wet it is, and whether we can get a trailer within a reasonable distance of the work.

What we can do is explain honestly what pushes a job to the top or the bottom of the scale, what a quote should cover before you accept it, and what questions to ask whoever you get one from — us included.

Why we don't publish a price list

You can find per-acre figures online in about ten seconds. They're worth almost nothing to you, because they're national averages built from thousands of jobs that look nothing like yours — flat ground in one state, hillside hardwood in another, all blended into one number.

Quote a stranger's average back to you and one of two things happens. Either the number is too low and it gets walked up once the crew is standing in your brush, or it's padded high enough to cover the worst case and you overpay for an easy job. Neither is how we want to start.

So instead of a number, here's the honest version: the things below are what a real estimator is adding up when they walk your property. Learn them and you'll be able to tell a serious quote from a guess — whoever it comes from.

The seven things that actually move the number

1. Stem size. The single biggest factor. An acre of briars and two-inch saplings and an acre of six-inch hardwood are not the same job and are nowhere near the same price.

2. Density. How much of it there is per square foot. Scattered brush moves fast; a wall of privet does not.

3. Slope. Steep ground slows a machine down and limits which direction it can work. Winston and Walker county hillsides cost more per acre than flat Pickens County bottomland, every time.

4. Ground moisture. Wet ground means waiting, or working slower to avoid tearing it up. A job priced in August and a job priced in February are not the same job.

5. Access. If a truck and lowboy can get within sight of the work, good. If the equipment has to walk half a mile in, that's time on the clock before anything gets cleared.

6. What happens to the debris. Mulched in place costs nothing extra. Windrowed and burned costs some. Loaded and hauled to a permitted site costs the most, and it's the line most often left off a cheap quote.

7. How finished you want it. Knocked down and walkable is one price. Stumps out, graded, seeded and ready to mow is another.

How to read a quote you didn't get from us

Ask whether debris removal is included or extra. Ask whether the price is for the whole job or a day rate that stops when the day does. Ask whether stumps are coming out or staying. Ask what happens if it rains for a week. Ask whether there's a mobilisation or transport charge on top.

A cheap number with four of those unanswered usually ends up more expensive than an honest one that covers them. That applies to our quotes as much as anybody's — if something on ours isn't clear, ask, and we'll put it in writing.

Getting a real number

Free, and no pressure attached. Tell us the county, roughly the acreage, what's growing on it and whether a trailer can get to it. If you're local we'll come look at it. If you're not, we can go over it with you on satellite and get close enough to be useful before anybody drives anywhere.

Straight Answers

Cost questions, answered straight

How much does forestry mulching cost per acre?

There is no honest per-acre number that fits every property, which is why you won't find one on this page. Light brush and two-inch saplings is a different job from heavy mixed hardwood, and the two are nowhere near each other on price. Any contractor who quotes you a per-acre figure before looking at the ground is guessing at your expense.

Is forestry mulching cheaper than traditional land clearing?

On the right job, yes, and usually by a wide margin — one machine instead of three, and nothing to load out. But mulching leaves the roots in the ground. If you need buildable ground, clearing costs more because it does more.

Why won't you quote a price over the phone?

Because the number would be wrong. Two properties the same size can be three times apart in price depending on stem size, slope, how wet the ground is, and whether a trailer can get near it. We'd rather look and be right.

What makes one job cost more than another?

Stem size and density more than anything. Then slope, ground moisture, access, whether stumps have to come out, what happens to the debris, and how much finish work you want at the end.

Is there a minimum charge?

Yes, on small jobs. Getting the equipment loaded, hauled, unloaded and back costs the same whether we're there two hours or two days. We'll tell you the minimum before you commit to anything.

Do you charge by the hour, the day, or the acre?

It depends on the job. Open-ended clean-up work often makes more sense hourly or daily; a defined tract with a clear edge is usually priced for the job. Whichever it is, you'll know which before we start.

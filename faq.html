FAQ

Questions people actually ask us

No sales answers. If the honest answer is "it depends," that's what it says.

About the work

What is forestry mulching?

A mulching head — a steel drum with carbide teeth — grinds standing brush and small trees where they stand and leaves the material on the ground as mulch. One machine, one pass, nothing hauled away.

Does mulching remove stumps?

No. It can take a stump flush with the ground, but the root ball stays. Stumps out means land clearing or excavating.

Will the brush grow back?

Some will. Cut stems on privet, sweetgum and kudzu send shoots up again. The mulch layer slows it considerably and a second pass is far quicker than the first. Anybody promising a permanent kill in one visit is guessing.

What size trees can you mulch?

Roughly six to eight inches on the stump on our machines. Bigger than that is a dozer job or a cut-then-mulch job, and we'll tell you which.

Forestry mulching or bush hogging?

A bush hog mows grass and soft stems. A mulcher grinds woody material. If a tractor can already drive across it, you want a bush hog.

What do I do with the mulch left behind?

Nothing, usually. It breaks down over a season or two and feeds the soil. If you're planting a food plot or seeding pasture it can be spread thin or pushed aside — say so before we start.

Scheduling, access and pricing

Do you charge for an estimate?

No.

How far out are you booking?

It moves with the weather and the schedule. Call or text and we'll give you an honest answer rather than a date we can't hold.

Do you have a minimum charge?

On small jobs, yes — hauling equipment there and back costs the same whether it's a two-hour job or a two-day one. You'll know the number before you commit.

Can you get to my property?

A tracked machine reaches a lot of places, but it still arrives on a trailer. Gates, bridges, low limbs and the first hundred feet of drive are what matter. Tell us about access when you call.

What areas do you cover?

Fayette, Walker, Tuscaloosa, Marion, Lamar, Pickens, Cullman and Winston counties. Just outside? Call anyway.

Permits, utilities and safety

Do I need a permit?

It depends on your county and whether the work touches a stream, a wetland or a right of way. We are not the permitting office and we won't tell you that you don't need one — check with your county before work starts.

Do you call in utility locates?

Always. Alabama 811 before any digging, on every job, including on private land.

What about asbestos in an old building?

Structures built before the early 1980s may contain it, and Alabama has rules about how it's handled. It needs testing before demolition. Tell us the age of the building when you call.

Can I burn the debris myself?

Burning brush on your own land is generally allowed with the right conditions and notification, and the Alabama Forestry Commission is who to ask. Construction and demolition debris is a different matter and shouldn't be burned.

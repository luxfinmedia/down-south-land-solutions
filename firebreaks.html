Firebreaks & Fire Lanes

Breaks cut around homes, shops, timber stands and property lines — before you need them, not during.

Get a Free Estimate Call (205) 535-6376

Why a break works

A fire moving through brush needs fuel in front of it. A break takes the fuel away in a strip wide enough that a ground fire runs out of anything to burn. It doesn't stop everything — nothing does in the wrong wind — but it buys time and it gives crews somewhere to work from.

Around a house, the first thirty feet matter most. Around timber, breaks want to follow the contours and tie into roads and creeks you already have.

Breaks and burning

If you're planning a prescribed burn, the break goes in first and it goes in wider than you think. The Alabama Forestry Commission has requirements and a notification process for burning, and that's a conversation to have with them, not with us. We cut the line; the burn is yours to plan.

Straight Answers

Firebreaks — questions we get asked

How wide should a firebreak be?

Wider than the flame length you expect, and wider on a slope than on flat ground. The Alabama Forestry Commission is the right place for specifics on your stand.

Can you cut breaks on steep ground?

Tracked machines handle a lot of slope, but not all of it. We'll walk it and tell you honestly if part of it is out of reach.

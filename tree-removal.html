Tree Removal in West Alabama

Dead, leaning, crowded or storm-damaged trees taken down and cleaned up. We work with machines on open ground, so if there's room to get to it, there's a way to get it out.

Get a Free Estimate Call (205) 535-6376

What we take down

Dead pines standing over a pasture. A leaning hardwood too close to the shop. Storm-broken tops hung up where nobody wants to walk under them. Trees crowding a drive, a fence line, a septic field or a building site. Volunteer trees that came up where they shouldn't have and are now big enough to matter.

Once it's down, you decide what happens to the wood: windrowed and burned on site, cut to length and stacked if you burn firewood, or hauled off. We price all three before anything hits the ground.

How we get it out

We're a land services outfit, not a climbing crew, and that shapes what we're good at. Our work is machine work — an excavator or a dozer with room to operate, felling from the ground, pushing, and lifting the material out in one piece where we can.

That makes us a strong, cheaper answer for open ground, pastures, tree lines, build sites and anywhere a machine can reach. It makes us the wrong answer for a tree that has to come out of a tight back yard in pieces, over a roof, with ropes. If your job is that job, we'll tell you on the phone and point you at somebody who climbs.

Stumps, and what's left after

Taking the tree is half of it. The stump and root ball stay in the ground unless somebody pulls them, and a yard full of stumps is still a yard you can't mow, disc or build on.

We pull stumps with an excavator, fill and pack the hole, and pull the ground back level so it isn't a trip hazard or a place water stands. If the job is a whole stand of trees rather than a handful, that's usually land clearing and it prices better that way — we'll say so.

Straight Answers

Tree Removal — questions we get asked

Do you take down trees close to a house?

Sometimes — it depends on what's around it and whether a machine can get to it safely. If the tree can be felled away from the structure with room to work, yes. If it has to come out in pieces over a roof, that's a climbing job and we'll tell you so rather than take a chance with your house.

Do you remove the stump too?

Yes, if you want it out. We pull the stump and root ball with an excavator, backfill the hole and level the ground. A stump ground flush is a different job — what we do is pull it out, which is what you want if anything is going on that ground afterward.

What happens to the wood?

Your call. Burned on site if the county and conditions allow, cut and stacked if you want the firewood, or hauled off. Burning is the cheapest and hauling is the cleanest.

Do you handle storm damage?

Yes. Downed and hung-up trees after a storm are a regular call for us. We can't promise same-day when the whole county is calling at once, but tell us what's on the ground and how urgent it is and we'll tell you straight where you land.

Is it cheaper to remove a few trees or clear the whole area?

Per tree, clearing is almost always cheaper — the machines are already there and set up. If you're looking at more than a handful of trees in one area, ask us to price it as land clearing as well and compare the two.

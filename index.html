Why Mulching

It stays on the ground where it fell

The difference between mulching and conventional clearing isn't the price. It's what's left when the machine leaves.

Nothing to haul off

The brush is ground where it stands. No piles sitting in your field waiting on a burn day, no roll-off container, no trucking bill buried at the bottom of the invoice.

The ground stays covered

That mulch layer holds your dirt through the first hard rain instead of letting a bare slope wash into the ditch. On a cut-and-pile job, erosion is a separate problem you pay to fix later.

One machine, one pass

No dozer, loader and dump truck all on the clock at once. That's why mulching opens ground up for less on the right job — and why we'll tell you straight when it's the wrong one.

Straight Answers

Common questions

What areas do you cover?

Fayette, Walker, Tuscaloosa, Marion, Lamar, Pickens, Cullman and Winston counties, out of Berry in Fayette County. If you're just outside that, call — the answer is often still yes.

Do you charge for an estimate?

No. We'll look at the property, or go over it with you on satellite if you're not local, and give you a number.

What's the difference between mulching and clearing?

Mulching grinds standing brush and small trees in place and leaves the roots. Clearing pushes timber and pulls the stumps out so you can build on it. Mulching is faster and cheaper; clearing gives you buildable ground. See our cost guide for how each one is priced.

How soon can you get to my property?

It depends on what's on the schedule and how wet it's been. Call or text and we'll tell you honestly where you'd fall, rather than promising a date we can't hold.
